# Anime sub/dub research

Status: Sources 29 and 30 are registered as additional direct anime sources, with the coverage and playback limitations documented below. Do not assert that every title has a dub or subtitles based on these samples.

## Existing multi-audio source (Source 28)

- Source 28 already maps TMDB show metadata to an AnimeSalt candidate, conservatively limited to season 1 and an exact episode-title match.
- [Real-player test](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44901067798): Naruto and Jujutsu Kaisen season 1 episodes 1 and 2 decoded video and non-silent audio while switching `ja → en → ja`. This tests the manifest track selection and playback, not independent recognition of the spoken language or the episode's identity. Metadata used in the test is a fixture.
- Each sample exposed zero subtitle tracks in that test. It is not known whether subtitles are burned in. Existing separately configured OpenSubtitles fallback may supply subtitles, but that was not verified here and is not guaranteed.
- The [catalogue survey](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44901402391) found playable-looking HLS candidates for Jujutsu Kaisen, My Hero Academia and Chainsaw Man. Some other searches failed or did not pass the conservative episode-title match. The survey uses TVMaze titles rather than genuine TMDB metadata; it is not a quantitative coverage estimate.

## Separate-stream candidate (Mikaza)

- The [discovery survey](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44901035007) used the existing TMDB→AniList mapping and tested five shows. English and Japanese requests returned distinct live URLs declared by the service as `dub` and `sub`, respectively. Those claims do not independently establish spoken language, episode identity, subtitle presence or availability for later seasons. No separate subtitle tracks were returned.
- The research-only injected prototype in `backend/test/anime-mikaza-ja-en-browser.mjs` is not registered in production. The [six-sample player test](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44901378830) did not pass the `ja → en → ja` switching check. In a [diagnostic](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44901750379) the first Japanese selection briefly reported success, but playback later ended up on an English source with a media network error. This does not prove a single cause. Separate-stream switching, media liveness, mapping and subtitles all need further investigation before promotion.
- AniList mapping currently has a known season/sequel ambiguity; do not assume TMDB season and episode numbers always match an AniList entry.

## Additional dedicated anime sources

No existing source was removed or changed during this research. A [dedicated-provider survey](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44903393296) evaluated four candidates via a pinned MIT-licensed Kuhi reference. Anikoto returned separate live HLS manifests for Naruto and Jujutsu Kaisen sub/dub requests. KAA returned the *same* URL for both requested languages in those samples, so its declared audio labels must not be trusted. AnimeGG returned redirects and Anineko raised errors. Liveness is not playback.

A [real-player Anikoto trial](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44903877466) injected individual candidates into the actual playback and relay stack, using fixture TMDB metadata and the Kuhi reference extractor. Naruto sub and dub both played and short samples were classified as Japanese and English. Attack on Titan dub passed; sub played and sampled as Japanese but timed out on the last of five later seeks. Jujutsu Kaisen sub and dub decoded initial video but both timed out on the seek. OCR with subtitle tracks disabled is inconclusive for burned-in dialogue; do not claim hard subtitles. Three shows and one run are not reliability proof. No provider was registered as a result.

A [real-player AnimeGG trial](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44904253399) got Attack on Titan sub and dub playing and classified short audio samples as Japanese and English. Naruto sub had no candidate and dub timed out; both Jujutsu Kaisen lanes timed out. OCR in several Attack on Titan Japanese frames found English-like text but cannot distinguish subtitles from other on-screen text; hard subtitles remain unconfirmed.

Anikoto's [server-group survey](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44904556557) found sub and dub groups, but no explicit hard-sub group for the first episodes of five anime. The already-bundled Anivexa Anikoto extractor [failed its conservative identity checks](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44904171692) for all three tested shows. Do not weaken that identity check just to make it return streams: incorrect sequel/episode mapping is a real risk. Kuhi's resolver is separate and is only being run in disposable research workflows; it is not a production dependency.

The [additional provider survey](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44904936177) found Reanime returned identical URLs for several sub/dub requests, Anizone returned Japanese candidates but no dub for three shows, and the other four extractors errored or produced no streams. These are discovery results, not proof of independent playback. A separate [hard-sub API probe](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44905065417) returned 403 or network errors; the sample slugs were not independently verified, and no streams were tested.

## Audio language verification

A repeatable optional automatic language diagnostic uses short ephemeral audio captured from the real player. It does not retain clips or transcripts, and detection is only supporting evidence: music, silence and multilingual speech can mislead it. Initial attempts failed due to a faster-whisper/PyAV API incompatibility; the research script decodes via ffmpeg instead. In the [corrected run](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44902154579), captured episode 1 English and Japanese tracks were classified as English and Japanese for both Naruto (0.953 and 0.983) and Jujutsu Kaisen (0.988 and 0.992). These are estimates from two short samples per show, not verification of entire tracks or of subtitles.

## NovusCloud and MegaPlay — raw-stream investigation

**Sources 29 and 30 are registered as additional direct-stream adapters.** They are restricted to Japanese-animation season-one episodes with exact normalized TMDB/AniZip episode-title matches; mismatches and later seasons fail closed. Set `ELFURA_ANIME_29_ENABLED=0` or `ELFURA_ANIME_30_ENABLED=0` to disable either. Existing source adapters are not replaced.

Both sources use a restricted HTTP dispatcher that validates IPv4 addresses at socket connection time, rejects private/reserved targets, uses HTTPS, bounds redirect hops and strips sensitive headers across origins. Novus resources are confined to its exact origin; MegaPlay resources can use public hosts because its segments rotate among multiple domains. These new lanes intentionally connect directly rather than through a Worker or HTTP proxy, since remote proxy DNS resolution cannot be pinned by this process. This may prevent them working on servers that require such egress. IPv6-only upstreams are currently unsupported. The MegaPlay extractor is a bounded fork of the attributed bundled extractor with downloaded-JavaScript execution and URL signing removed; provider changes requiring signing may fail.

[Novus test 44909956189](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44909956189) passed sub→dub→sub for Naruto 1–2 and Jujutsu Kaisen 1 and showed English subtitles for the first episodes. [MegaPlay test 44911036429](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44911036429) passed sub→dub→sub and visible English subtitles for Naruto 1 and Jujutsu Kaisen 1; Naruto 2 sub played but switching to dub timed out repeatedly. This is a **known unresolved playback limitation**, not proof that all dub streams work. The provider's hard-sub label remains unverified; subtitles seen in the test were selectable soft subtitles. TMDB metadata in these live tests is a fixture, not actual TMDB service coverage. No real production deployment was verified.

The workflow/result identity was checked against the workflow files at the exact run commits and against annotation titles: [44906046394](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44906046394) is **Novus**, and [44906080429](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44906080429) is **MegaPlay**. Earlier notes that reversed those run identities were wrong.

- Novus: the raw API returned live HLS for first-episode sub/dub for Naruto, Jujutsu Kaisen and Attack on Titan, and Naruto episode 2 hsub. In run 44906046394 these played through the player and relay; short audio samples were classified Japanese for the sub/hsub samples and English for the dub samples. The hsub-labelled Naruto episode 2 yielded a little text by OCR, but this does **not** establish that dialogue subtitles are burned in. The hsub route was unavailable for the tested first episodes. Provider mode labels are claims, not guarantees.
- MegaPlay: run 44906080429 played first-episode Naruto, Jujutsu Kaisen and Attack on Titan sub/dub plus Naruto episode 2 sub/dub through the relay and player. Short samples were classified Japanese and English except Naruto episode 2 dub, which had no spoken segments and was inconclusive. The research-only restricted wrapper prevented downloaded signing code from being executed, but this wrapper is not production-hardening. Extracted VTT tracks were not injected in that test.
- In the [Novus prototype integration trial](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44907046172), fixture TMDB metadata and real upstream requests drove the real adapter, playback API, relay and browser player. Naruto episodes 1–2 and Jujutsu Kaisen episode 1 switched sub→dub→sub with video, non-silent audio and plausible episode duration. A subsequent [restricted-origin trial](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44908650353) also checked that an English cue was visible in the actual player overlay for both episode-1 samples. This establishes selectable soft subtitles for those samples, not synchronization with dialogue or burned-in subtitles. A separate run timed out on one of the three samples, so reliability is not proven. The trials use fixture metadata and do not establish real TMDB coverage or provider URL expiry behavior.
- The [AniZip mapping comparison](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44906105654) used exact normalized titles and fixture TMDB episode names. It matched Naruto 1–2, Jujutsu Kaisen 1–2 and One Piece 1; Attack on Titan 1 did not match and the negative episode was absent. The prototype intentionally rejects later seasons, non-Japanese/non-animation metadata, missing titles, and mismatches; this also produces false negatives. Exact episode titles reduce ambiguity but do not prove provider video identity.

## Production-path follow-up

The prior MegaPlay Naruto E2 dub failure was traced to MPEG-TS segments served with `text/html` content type: the relay treated the header as proof of an HTML error. The [format inspection](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44915368611) detected packet sync in the binary payload. The restricted-media relay now admits those responses only after checking multiple TS packet boundaries, still rejects actual HTML and HTML-labelled keys, and streams non-PNG image-labelled segments rather than buffering the complete segment. The [real-TMDB browser run](https://github.com/themithology/Elfura/actions/runs/44915601597) subsequently passed sub→dub→sub, video and non-silent audio for Naruto E1/E2 and Jujutsu Kaisen E1 for both Sources 29 and 30, including the formerly failing Source 30 Naruto E2. Source 28 passed playback and Hindi/English switching for Naruto and Jujutsu Kaisen E1/E2, and its manifest reported Japanese among its tracks; that run did not switch or classify Japanese speech. These are samples, not proof every episode, audio language or deployment works. Soft subtitles were observed for E1; burned-in subtitles remain unverified. Another real-TMDB run had intermittent failures, so upstream reliability is not guaranteed.

Fixed two independent late-join defects: a balanced remote pool could send a player's late request to a different resolver from the one with its in-memory results, and a source-specific recovery could clear those results. Routing affinity is bounded and media-node-local; recovery no longer clears the shared stash. Unit and media-server integration regression tests exercise these cases. A remote resolver timeout still requires deployment-specific investigation; this change does not establish its cause.
